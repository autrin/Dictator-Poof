# Creative direction — living beyond the rules

Working title: **Dictator Poof**. Genre: first-person action with comic pursuit
and unsettling environments. This document contains future ideas as well as
the implemented prototype; it is not a feature-completion claim.

## Emotional purpose

Express the frustration of having ordinary life controlled by arbitrary
authority, and the relief of recovering agency. Inspiration includes
documented repression under the Islamic Republic of Iran. The playable
antagonists and the Department of No are fictional.

The core rhythm is **unease → pursuit → absurdity → defiance → visible relief**.
Authorities are the butt of the joke. Civilians, queer people, religious or
nonreligious communities, and people surviving oppression are not punchlines.
Combat opponents are active fictional armed adversaries, not ordinary people
classified as targets for their beliefs.

## Implemented first slice

A dark censorship office with oversized, wobbling guards. Restore three
blocked relay terminals, evade or fight the pursuers, and reach the uplink.
The victory screen acknowledges a successful broadcast. Small blood effects
are optional. No gore, dismemberment, or real-person likenesses are included.

## Strong next experiment: the rule machine

Make an announcer issue increasingly absurd rules during a run. Rules should
change gameplay in readable ways, not merely add text. A player can sabotage
the machine to turn a rule against its enforcers. One example: a "no running"
order causes the guards' own stamp machines to reject them mid-chase.

Prototype one rule, one clear warning, one consequence, and one reversal.
Do not build dozens of rules before proving that a single reversal is fun.

## Make winning change the world

Future levels should restore something tangible rather than only increase a
kill count. A liberated hub could show people meeting freely, speaking without
censorship, playing music, drinking at a bar, and forming same-sex relationships
without persecution. Queer characters should have ordinary warmth and agency.
Quiet moments let the player experience what they were fighting for.

Use a clear before/after contrast: interrupted phone calls become connected,
shuttered gathering places reopen, and surveillance announcements give way to
conversation. Fear of war can inform the atmosphere without turning civilian
suffering into spectacle. Specific personal stories need their own adaptation
discussion before being put into scenes.

## Future fictional cast

- The Approval Minister: issues mutually contradictory permissions.
- The Censor: redacts words so aggressively that orders stop making sense.
- The Acting Deputy Assistant: insists he is in charge while fleeing responsibility.
- The Cabinet: a later ensemble of rival bureaucrats with different mechanics.

These are sketches, not implemented bosses. Real political figures can be
discussed in sourced context or nonviolent satire; the action cast here remains
fictional. Use original art and properly licensed material.

## Scope and playtesting

First complete a reliable five-minute loop. Next, improve pursuit, audio cues,
and one memorable rule reversal. Then replace primitive art. Add story scenes
and a liberated hub only after the core encounter is fun.

Virality cannot be promised. A useful first test is five people playing without
instructions: can they name the objective, understand why they lost, laugh at
one moment, and want another attempt? Ask what moment they would show a friend.
Do not add multiplayer or an open world to solve a weak core loop.
