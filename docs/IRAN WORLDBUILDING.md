# Iran — Worldbuilding & Reference Bible

**Purpose:** Source material for the game set in / about Iran. Written as reference context, and to onboard contributors.

**How to use this file:**
- Sections 1–2 are the *lens*. Read them first. Everything else is detail that only makes sense through that lens.
- Sections 3–7 are factual/historical grounding.
- Sections 8–13 are culture, language, and texture.
- Sections 14–16 are editorial rules, mechanic ideas, and glossary.
- Where a number or claim is contested, it is marked. Do not strip those markers.

**A note on accuracy:** Parts of this draw on advocacy writing, where figures circulate that independent monitors have not confirmed. Those are flagged inline. If the game asserts a number, use the monitored one and cite it. Getting this wrong hands ammunition to people who want to dismiss the whole thing.

## 1. The Core Lens — read this before anything else

These are the framing commitments this project is built on. They are not neutral, and they are not pretending to be.

**1. Iran ≠ the Islamic Republic.** This is the single most important distinction in the entire project. The Islamic Republic is a regime that took power in 1979 and governs a country it does not represent. Iran is a civilization roughly three thousand years older than it. Writing that collapses the two — "Iran did X," "Iranians believe Y" when meaning the state — is the primary failure mode to avoid. Use "the Islamic Republic," "the regime," "the IRGC," "the state." Reserve "Iran" and "Iranians" for the country and its people.

**2. Islam was imposed on Iran, twice.** Once by the Arab conquest in the 7th century (the period Zarrinkoub called *دو قرن سکوت* — "Two Centuries of Silence"), and again in 1979 when a constitution was written with Islamic jurisprudence as its foundation. The resentment in this project is directed at a *political-legal system*, not at Muslims as people. That distinction matters and should be visible in the writing — but the project does not soften its position on the system itself.

**3. There is no reformist wing.** Pezeshkian, Zarif, Rouhani, Khatami — these are branches of the same structure. "Reform from within" is treated here as a category error, not a competing good-faith position. The system's constitution, its Guardian Council vetting, and its Supreme Leader make electoral reform structurally impossible by design.

**4. The IRGC *is* the regime.** Not its army — its skeleton. Analysts across the political spectrum converge on this: removing the Supreme Leader does not remove the system, because the Revolutionary Guards hold the economy, the security apparatus, and the succession machinery.

**5. Iranians have fought, repeatedly, with their bare hands.** 2009. 2017. 2019. 2022. 2026. Each time they were shot, arrested, tortured, and hanged. Any framing that treats Iranians as passive, or as needing to be saved, is wrong. They have been trying for decades, under conditions designed to make trying lethal.

**6. Target the oppressor, not the nation.** A recurring position in this material: the regime's military, IRGC bases, Basij headquarters, and nuclear sites are one category. Bridges, power grids, hospitals, water systems, schools, and civilians are another, and striking them punishes the hostages and strengthens the hostage-taker's narrative.

**7. Grief and celebration happen at the same time.** The emotional register of this project is not pure anger. It is a Nowruz table set with a heavy heart. Mourning and continuity, in the same gesture. If the game is only rage, it is missing the point.

**8. Pre-Islamic Persia is a living identity, not nostalgia.** Cyrus, the Achaemenids, Zoroastrian fire, the Shahnameh, Nowruz — these are load-bearing parts of how many Iranians understand themselves *now*, precisely because the regime has spent decades trying to subordinate them to an Arab-Islamic identity.

**9. Lived experience is not a prerequisite for caring.** A note on how to pitch this: you don't have to be Iranian to care about censorship, internet freedom, or human rights. The project should be legible and compelling to people with no connection to Iran. Identity is context, not credential.

**10. Nobody is coming.** A hard-earned theme. Western statements of "concern" produce nothing. Progressive movements that mobilize for other causes have been largely silent on Iran, in part because the Islamic Republic funds groups those movements support. That silence is itself part of the Iranian experience of the last decades and belongs in the game.

## 2. The Emotional Texture

What it actually feels like, which matters more for a game than the political analysis:

- **Double life.** Public self and private self are different people. What you wear, say, drink, listen to, and who you love outside has to be edited. Inside the home, something else entirely. Nearly every Iranian under the Islamic Republic runs this split.
- **Ambient surveillance.** Not cameras everywhere — the knowledge that a neighbor, a classmate, a Basiji on a motorcycle, or a van at the end of the street could end your life's trajectory today. The threat is intermittent and unpredictable, which is what makes it effective.
- **The blackout as emotional event, not technical one.** When the internet goes down, it is not inconvenience. It is: *I cannot reach my mother. I do not know if my cousin is alive. Nobody outside can see what is being done to us.* Shutdowns and massacres arrive together, and everyone knows it.
- **Watching from outside, helpless.** The diaspora experience: being in a safe country, refreshing a dead feed, unable to call, able to do nothing but post. This is its own distinct kind of anguish and is underrepresented in fiction.
- **Islamotrauma.** A term used in this material: the specific, cumulative psychological damage of growing up under a system where religious law governs your body, your clothing, your relationships, and your speech — and where the punishment for being yourself is lashes, prison, or a rope.
- **Joy as defiance.** Dancing, music, a Haft-Sin table, a Yalda night, cooking a meal, a poem — under this system these are political acts. The regime understood this, which is why it attacked them.
- **Exhaustion.** Forty-seven years (1979–2026). Generations have now lived and died entirely inside this. There is deep fatigue underneath the defiance.

## 3. Timeline — Modern Iran

**Deep background**
- Achaemenid Empire (Cyrus, Darius) — the civilizational reference point.
- 7th century CE — Arab conquest; Islam imposed. Ferdowsi writes the *Shahnameh* (c. 1010 CE), preserving the Persian language and mythology.
- 1079 CE — Omar Khayyam leads the reform of the Jalali calendar, calculating the solar year to within seconds of modern measurement.

**Constitutional era**
- 1905–11 — Constitutional Revolution (*مشروطه*). Iranians genuinely building a constitutional, modernizing state. Chronicled by Ahmad Kasravi, who was later assassinated by the Islamist group Fada'iyan-e Islam.

**Pahlavi era (1925–1979)** — see Section 6 for detail.
- 1935 — Reza Shah formally asks foreign governments to use "Iran" rather than "Persia." Note: this was *not* a change of the country's internal name. It had always been Iran domestically — Old Persian *Aryānām* → Middle Persian *Ērān* → Sassanid *Ērānšahr*. "Persia" was an external Greco-Latin term from the province of Pars/Fars. In the 1950s both names were permitted in foreign-language use.
- 1953 — CIA/MI6-backed coup removes Mossadegh. Derails Iran's democratic trajectory long before 1979.
- 1963 — White Revolution: land reform, women's suffrage, Literacy Corps.
- 1971 — Seizure of Abu Musa and the Greater/Lesser Tunb islands. **Correction worth preserving:** this was a military operation with casualties, not a purely diplomatic achievement, and remains disputed with the UAE.
- 1979 — Revolution. A broad coalition — secular liberals, leftists, democrats, nationalists, Islamists — overthrows the Shah. Khomeini consolidates power and turns on nearly all of them. **The central tragedy: most people who made the revolution did not want what came next.**

**Islamic Republic (1979–present)**
- 1981–85 — 7,900+ political prisoners executed (vs. fewer than 100 political executions 1971–79 under the Shah).
- 1988 — Khomeini's fatwa triggers the mass execution of political prisoners; estimates up to 30,000, including children as young as 13.
- 2009 — Green Movement. Disputed election. Mass protests, crushed. The Islamic Republic killed a lot of people.
- 2017–18 — Economic protests spread across small cities and towns.
- Nov 2019 — "Bloody November." Fuel price protests. **1,500+ killed under a near-total national internet blackout.** This is the template: shutdown first, massacre under cover of it.
- Jan 2020 — IRGC shoots down Ukrainian flight PS752, killing 176, then lies about it for days.
- Sept 2022 — Mahsa (Jina) Amini dies in morality police custody. **Woman, Life, Freedom** erupts nationwide. Internet shut down again. 5-10 thousands killed, ~19,000 arrested. One of the most serious challenges the regime has faced.
- Jun 2025 — "Twelve-Day War." Israeli/US strikes on nuclear infrastructure. ~22,000 centrifuges destroyed per FDD estimates.
- **Jan 8 – late May 2026 — The 88-day national internet blackout. The longest documented nationwide internet disruption ever recorded anywhere. Islamic Republic killed ~40,000 people in 2 days.** People had a few cities in control for some time and Trump promised support. See Section 5 for internet details.
- Feb 28, 2026 — Operation Epic Fury begins. Supreme Leader Khamenei killed on the opening day. Strait of Hormuz closed. Hezbollah and the Houthis drawn in.
- Mar 2026 — War ongoing. Expert consensus: regime weakened but surviving. Casualty figures contested.
- May 26, 2026 — Partial internet restoration begins. What returned was not the internet — it was a whitelist. The internet is actually a filternet.

## 4. The Architecture of Control

Mechanically rich. Most of this translates directly into game systems.

**گشت ارشاد — Gasht-e Ershad (Morality Police).** Patrol vans and foot teams enforcing Islamic dress and conduct codes. They stop women over hair showing, tight clothing, visible ankles. They stop couples. **You cannot walk with a girlfriend or boyfriend before marriage. You cannot hold hands. You cannot kiss in public.** Arrests happen for sitting in a park with someone you are not married to. Detainees report threats, including threats of rape, as a routine intimidation tool. Mahsa Amini died in their custody.

**بسیج — Basij.** Paramilitary volunteer militia under the IRGC. The regime's street-level enforcement body. Motorcycles, batons, live ammunition at protests. Embedded in universities, workplaces, and neighborhoods — so the surveillance is social, not just technical. Sarina Esmailzadeh was beaten to death by Basij forces.

**سپاه پاسداران — IRGC (Revolutionary Guards).** Not a conventional military. Controls major sectors of the economy, the missile and nuclear programs, foreign proxy operations, and internal security. Estimated regime hardcore: 12+ million people, including perhaps 500,000 adult men willing to fight to preserve the system. This is the obstacle that makes decapitation strategies fail.

**وزارت اطلاعات — Ministry of Intelligence (Ettela'at).** Surveillance, infiltration, interrogation, forced televised confessions.

**The legal code.** Islamic jurisprudence is the foundation of the constitution. Consequences:
- Apostasy / publicly declaring atheism: capital offense.
- Same-sex conduct: lashing, imprisonment, or execution.
- Mandatory hijab, enforced by law from age 9.
- Legal testimony and inheritance weighted against women.
- Being outed as queer or trans at a university means expulsion.
- Selling VPNs: arrests documented (one on 7 April 2026 per wire reports).
- **Starlink possession: up to 10 years in prison; per 2026 legislation, potentially execution.**

**Prisons.** Evin is the best known. Documented methods under the Islamic Republic include torture inherited from SAVAK plus additions: the "coffin" (cubicles roughly 50cm × 80cm × 140cm), forced watching of executions, sexual violence in detention. The UN has confirmed systematic rape and gang rape including of children as young as 12; Amnesty documented 45 cases of sexual violence across 17 provinces during the 2022 crackdown.

**The standard sequence, which the game should treat as a known pattern:**
> Protest begins → internet is cut → mass killing happens in the dark → bodies are withheld or buried secretly → families are forced onto state television to recant → connectivity returns → the regime denies everything → monitors reconstruct the death toll weeks later.

## 5. The Internet Shutdown — The Technical Heart

This is the richest mechanical seam in the whole project. A censorship regime is a *rule system*, and rule systems are games.

### 5.1 The 2026 blackout and what came back

- **Jan 8 → late May 2026. 88 days.** Longest nationwide shutdown ever recorded.
- What returned is **not** "the internet, but censored." It is a **national-scale protocol whitelist at the network edge** — a per-destination privilege system.
- ~90 million people cut off; a tiny tier (order of 100K) has elevated access.

### 5.2 How the whitelist actually works — three layers

**Layer 1 — Protocol fingerprinting.** When a TCP connection leaves Iran, the firewall inspects the **first one or two packets**. If they don't match a DNS, HTTP, or HTTPS fingerprint, the flow is silently blackholed. SSH, custom protocols, most VPN handshakes — gone. (Documented by the Geneva research group at UMD. *Note: a "6 packets per connection" figure circulates in Persian community channels; it is single-sourced and unverified — do not use it.*)

**Layer 2 — SNI allowlisting.** In a TLS handshake, the ClientHello is **not encrypted** and contains the `server_name` (SNI) field in plaintext. The censor reads that one field. Wrong SNI → connection dies. Right SNI on a whitelisted IP → unrestricted bandwidth. The censor never has to decrypt anything.
  - ESNI/ECH (Encrypted Client Hello) would fix this — so Iran blocks connections that use it, because using it is itself a circumvention signal.

**Layer 3 — Transit chokepointing.** Iran rerouted a large share of outbound traffic through Delta Telecom in Azerbaijan (AS29049). Side effect: Azerbaijan's per-capita ChatGPT usage jumped from roughly #44 in the world to **#3**, climbing 41 positions. Those were Iranians. From the outside they looked Azerbaijani; from the regime's side, every external connection now ran through a chokepoint they controlled more tightly. *(Triple-sourced: Kentik, Digital Impact Lab, and Persian technical channels.)*

### 5.3 The arms race, in order

A beautiful escalation ladder for a game's progression system:

1. **Blocklist era.** Censor blocks known bad SNIs. Users use VPNs.
2. **SNI Spoofing.** Make the censor read a *whitelisted* SNI while the real server receives the real one. Worked well against Cloudflare-fronted configs. Requires a desktop and admin rights.
3. **Censor counters with stateful NAT at the national border** — rewriting TCP sequence numbers and checksums. This kills SNI Spoofing outright.
4. **DNS tunneling becomes the survivor.** DNS stays in the whitelist because the regime *cannot disable DNS without breaking the domestic intranet it actually wants functioning.* Payloads are encoded into subdomain labels; responses come back through TXT records. Throughput is terrible. Latency is worse. It works.
   - Tools: **DNSTT**, NoizDNS, VayDNS, SlipStream, SlipNet, MasterDns.
5. **Post-restoration (current):** what carries real traffic now is **whitelisted-SNI proxying through CDNs** — VLESS/Trojan over WebSocket, Cloudflare-fronted. DNS tunneling was the fallback during the *worst weeks of total blackout*, not the present main channel. **This correction matters — the common version of this story gets it wrong.**

### 5.4 Protocol family reference

- **Shadowsocks (SS)** — the original, from China, 2012. Encrypted SOCKS5 proxy. Traffic looks like random bytes. Weakness: passive DPI classifies "not HTTP, not TLS, high entropy → probably SS." China's GFW has done this since ~2015; Iran does too.
  URI form: `ss://BASE64(method:password)@host:port#name`
- **VMess / VLESS** — the v2ray family. VMess is older; VLESS is slimmer. Supports many transports underneath (WebSocket, gRPC, TCP).
- **Trojan** — disguises itself as ordinary HTTPS to a real website.
- **REALITY** — borrows a genuine third-party TLS certificate handshake so active probing sees a legitimate site. **VLESS + REALITY** is a current strong configuration.
- **MTProto proxies** — Telegram-only access. Narrow, but Telegram is where Iranian information actually lives.
- **Psiphon Conduit** — diaspora bandwidth donation. You run a node on a spare device outside Iran; each node carries roughly 25 concurrent users. When partial restoration began on **May 26, Psiphon's daily users inside Iran jumped from 782,000 to 4.23 million in 24 hours.**
- **Tor Snowflake** — secondary option.
- **uTLS** — TLS fingerprint mimicry, so your client's handshake looks like Chrome instead of v2ray.
- Client apps seen in use: **Hiddify, v2rayNG, v2rayN, Nekoray, Karing, sing-box, SlipNet, Psiphon, شیر و خورشید / AzadiTunnel, DefyxVPN, Geph, MahsaNG.**

### 5.5 The lived reality of using these

**Critical for game feel.** Connections do not simply work or fail. They **pulse**. They connect, carry traffic for a minute, drop. Users rotate through four, five, six different apps in a session looking for one that holds. A video call connects and dies mid-sentence. This is confirmed by Filterwatch's June 2026 report and independent reporting. Any depiction of "turn on VPN → internet works" is wrong.

### 5.6 The chicken-and-egg problem

**One of the best game-design seeds in this entire document.** To get circumvention tools you need the internet. To get the internet you need circumvention tools.

Real solution devised for someone traveling into Iran:
- A cheap, dedicated travel Android device carried in clean.
- APK installers and config strings on a **separate microSD card in luggage**, so the device itself is clean at the border.
- Offline installation on arrival.
- Primary phone stripped of circumvention apps, logged out of sensitive Telegram channels, before crossing.

### 5.7 Economics of access (2026)

- Internet Pro activation: ~2,178,000 toman (~$30) plus per-GB charges.
- Smuggled Starlink kit: $3,000–$4,000 (and a potential death sentence).
- "Observer bots" on Telegram claiming to show your data usage: scams.
- The pricing is deliberate. **It normalizes tiered access — turning connectivity from a utility into a purchasable privilege.**

### 5.8 Operational security rules (as actually given)

- Never use random Telegram-channel configs for banking, email, or anything sensitive. You do not know who runs the exit node.
- Distinguish regime-aligned channels from independent technical voices. Both exist and both look technical.
- Using circumvention tools privately is tolerated in practice but is not legal. Selling them gets you arrested.

### 5.9 The numbers station — V32 "Tavajjoh"

Pure game material, and real.

- First logged **February 28, 2026**, roughly 12 hours after the first strikes.
- Broadcasts on shortwave **7910 kHz**, later also **7842 kHz** after jamming.
- Twice daily, early morning and early evening UTC.
- A man's voice reads strings of numbers **in Persian**. Numbers read twice. A pause. Then the word **توجه — *tavajjoh*, "attention" — spoken three times.** Then more number sequences.
- Dual-tone beeps in early transmissions match the PT override warning tone on L3/Harris radios — automatically injected when a radio configured for cipher text transmits in plaintext. This implies a Harris Falcon II/III, equipment Iranians would not readily have.
- Radio hobbyists triangulated the transmitter to a region covering northern Italy, Switzerland, western Germany, eastern France, Belgium, and the Netherlands.
- Most likely a Western intelligence service (CIA or Mossad) signalling agents inside Iran. With the internet down, a Cold War technology came back to life.

## 6. The Pahlavi Era — What Was Built, and What Was Wrong

Handle honestly. Flattening this into either hagiography or dismissal is a failure.

### Reza Shah (r. 1925–1941)
- Unified a fragmented, semi-feudal state; ended the power of regional warlords.
- Built the **Trans-Iranian Railway** — Persian Gulf to Caspian Sea — financed entirely with national revenue, refusing foreign loans to preserve sovereignty.
- Founded the **University of Tehran** (1934) and hundreds of schools, including girls' schools.
- Replaced clerical Sharia courts with a secular civil code on the French model.
- Mandated birth certificates, standardized surnames, modernized the civil registry.
- Banned the veil in 1936 — a coercive act, and should be named as such, even by people who support secularism.
- First modern industrial factories (textiles, sugar, cement); thousands of km of roads.

### Mohammad Reza Shah (r. 1941–1979)
- **White Revolution (1963):** land reform breaking up feudal estates; **women's suffrage**; Literacy Corps (*سپاه دانش*); Health Corps (*سپاه بهداشت*); worker profit-sharing; nationalization of forests and water.
- **Family Protection Law** — reformed marriage, divorce, custody. Women entered universities, the workforce, the judiciary, parliament.
- Literacy rose from roughly **10% in 1940 to 85% among urban youth by 1976.**
- Founded Shiraz, Isfahan, Sharif universities; thousands of state scholarships abroad.
- Infrastructure: highways, dams (Dez, Karaj), rural electrification, Iran Air, Tehran metro planning.
- Industrialization: steel, petrochemicals, automotive. GDP growth among the world's highest in the 1960s–70s. Founding OPEC member.
- Invested in pre-Islamic heritage (the Persepolis celebrations).
- Began the nuclear program (Bushehr).

### The honest other side
- **SAVAK.** Secret police. Arbitrary arrest, systematic torture, suppression of all political opposition.
- **Fewer than 100 political prisoners executed 1971–1979** — a real number, and far smaller than what followed, but not zero.
- One-party Rastakhiz system: no meaningful political participation.
- Modernization imposed without democratic consent, which generated its own backlash.
- Perceived foreign dependency, corruption, and inequality concentrated among elites.

### The comparison, stated plainly
The Shah's regime was authoritarian with documented abuses. The Islamic Republic's record is worse by orders of magnitude in scale of execution and systematic repression. Both of those statements are true simultaneously, and the game is stronger for holding both.

## 7. The Names — The Memory Bank

Victims of the Woman, Life, Freedom crackdown and the regime's repression. These names carry the movement. Treat them with care; they were real people.

- **Mahsa (Jina) Amini**, 22 — died in morality police custody, September 2022. The spark.
- **Nika Shakarami**, 16 — burned her headscarf in Tehran. Disappeared for 10 days. Found in a prison morgue, skull shattered, nose destroyed. The IRGC took her body at night and buried it secretly 40km away, then forced her family onto state television.
- **Sarina Esmailzadeh**, 16 — a YouTuber. Beaten to death with batons by Basij forces. The regime told her mother she jumped off a roof. Amnesty confirmed she was beaten to death.
- **Hadis Najafi**, 22 — shot six times at close range: face, neck, heart. Her recorded last words: that she liked to think years later she'd be glad she joined the protest.
- **Armita Abbasi** — gang-raped repeatedly in detention by IRGC agents. A leaked government document confirmed the perpetrators and ordered the case classified "completely secret."
- **Aida Rostami** — a paramedic. Abducted for treating wounded protesters. Eye gouged, hand crushed, body discarded.
- **Asra Panahi**, 15 — beaten to death for refusing to sing a pro-regime anthem.
- **Diana Mahmoudi**, 7 — shot.
- **Hosna Bameri**, 3 — killed.
- **Narges Mohammadi** — Nobel Peace Prize laureate. 44-year sentence. Beaten. Hunger strikes. For refusing silence.
- **The 176 of PS752** — shot down by the IRGC in January 2020; the state lied for days.

### On casualty figures — read before using numbers
Figures for Jan 8-9, 2026 — the initial days of the national internet blackout and the regime's crackdown:
- **Casualties:** Approximately 40,000 killed by the Islamic Republic in 2 days.
- **Injuries:** Tens of thousands more injured.
- **Displacement:** Hundreds of thousands displaced internally.
- **Infrastructure damage:** Widespread damage to residential units and public facilities.
The regime shot people using automatic guns and even killed already injured patients by shooting them in the head in the hospitals.
People were scared to tell the providers they were shot, fearing the regime would arrest and kill them.
- **Hanging:** Reports of a lot of people being hanged by the regime after the crackdown.

## 9. Persian Culture & Identity

### نوروز — Nowruz
The Persian New Year, at the **exact moment of the vernal equinox** — the instant the Sun crosses the celestial equator. Not a date on a calendar: a measured astronomical event. Over 3,000 years old.

- Tradition attributes its founding to **Jamshid (جمشید)**, the mythological king who discovered the turning of the seasons — hence *نوروز جمشیدی*.
- In **1079 CE, Omar Khayyam** led astronomers who calculated the solar year to within seconds of modern measurement and reformed the **Jalali calendar** to align perfectly with the equinox.
- The point worth making: Iranians marked the new year by **observation and mathematics**, not decree.

**هفت‌سین — The Haft-Sin.** Seven items beginning with the letter س:

| Item | Meaning |
|---|---|
| سبزه (sabzeh) — sprouted wheat/barley/lentil | rebirth, renewal. Grown from seed ~2 weeks ahead. The centerpiece. |
| سمنو (samanu) — sweet wheat germ pudding | affluence, sweetness. Traditionally cooked by women gathered through the night. |
| سنجد (senjed) — dried oleaster | love |
| سیر (sir) — garlic | medicine, health |
| سیب (sib) — apple, red | beauty, health |
| سماق (somāq) — sumac | the color of sunrise, patience |
| سرکه (serkeh) — vinegar | age, patience, transformation |

Accompaniments: **آینه** (mirror — reflection, seeing the year truly, placed at the back); **شمع** (candles, one per family member — descends directly from Zoroastrian fire veneration); **تخم‌مرغ رنگی** (painted eggs — fertility); **سکه** (coins — prosperity); **سنبل** (hyacinth — the smell of Nowruz itself); **نارنج** (a bitter orange floating in water — the earth suspended in space); **ماهی قرمز** (goldfish — life, though many Iranians now skip it for animal welfare reasons).

**The book.** A book of wisdom goes on the sofreh. **دیوان حافظ** is the most culturally canonical — families perform **فال حافظ** at the moment of تحویل سال. The **شاهنامه** is the other strong choice, and ties directly to pre-Islamic identity. Rumi (مولانا / Jalal ad-Din Mohammad Balkhi) belongs too — born in Balkh in Greater Khorasan, inside the Persian civilizational sphere, and he wrote entirely in Persian.

**Historical note:** some scholars hold the tradition was originally **هفت‌شین** (Haft-Shin, seven items with ش), predating the Arab conquest, and shifted to سین over the centuries.

### شاهنامه — The Shahnameh
Ferdowsi's epic, c. 1010 CE. **The soul of Iranian identity, preserved in language when nearly everything else was taken.** Rostam, Esfandiyar, Zal and Rudabeh, Siyavash, Jamshid, Zahhak and Kaveh the blacksmith — a complete mythic cycle available for adaptation.

**Verification rule — learned the hard way.** Verses from the Shahnameh must be checked on **Ganjoor (ganjoor.net)** before use. Plausible-sounding Shahnameh verses are extremely easy to fabricate or misremember, and fabricating one and attributing it to Ferdowsi is a serious error. A verified example:

> بخندید و گفت ای برادر تو خوان / بیارای و آزادگان را بخوان
> *— Rostam and Esfandiyar, section 13, verse 33 — ganjoor.net/ferdousi/shahname/esfandyar/sh13*
> (A pun: the first *خوان* is the spread/table; *بخوان* is to invite. Hospitality, setting the table, calling the free-born.)

Authoritative English translations: **Dick Davis** (*Shahnameh: The Persian Book of Kings*, Penguin 2006), the **Warner brothers** (1905–25), **Jerome Clinton** (*In the Dragon's Claws*, 1999). Do not invent a translation and present it as published.

### Names and language
- **آترین (Atrin)** — authentic Persian/Avestan, from *ātar/āzar* (fire). "Guardian of fire" / "fire-dweller." Appears in Shahnameh mythology.
- **سیمین (Simin)** — "silvery," from *sim* (silver). Bearers: Simin Daneshvar, Simin Behbahani.
- **آنیتا (Anita)** — most likely Spanish/European (diminutive of Ana ← Hebrew Hannah, "grace"). Persian sources sometimes link it to Anahita; these folk etymologies lack academic support. Worth knowing as an example of how identity-reclamation can overreach.
- **نادیا (Nadia)** — primarily Slavic (from Nadezhda, "hope"); a parallel Arabic form means "one who calls out."

**On vocabulary:** there is a strong preference in this project for Persian words over Arabic loanwords. Prefer **سپاس** over تشکر, **درود** over سلام, **پاسخ** over جواب, **باشنده** over موجود, where it doesn't become stilted. This is a live cultural-political position, not pedantry — it's about reclaiming a language that was partly overwritten.

### Food
Persian cooking as cultural continuity, and vegan-adaptable throughout:
- **آش رشته** (ash reshteh) — noodle soup; nearly vegan already (skip the kashk). Kidney beans, chickpeas, lentils, spinach, herbs, turmeric.
- **قورمه سبزی** (ghormeh sabzi) — herb stew. Vegan with extra kidney beans; mountains of parsley, cilantro, fenugreek (*شنبلیله*), chives.
- **عدس پلو** (adas polo) — lentil rice with caramelized onion, raisins, cinnamon, cumin.
- **لوبیا پلو** (loobia polo) — green bean rice; TVP or crumbled tofu with tomato paste, turmeric, cinnamon.
- **خورشت بادمجان** (khoresht bademjan) — eggplant stew with *limoo amani* (dried lime), saffron, turmeric, cinnamon. TVP is the best meat substitute here — it absorbs the sauce the way beef chunks do.
- **میرزا قاسمی** (mirza ghasemi) — smoked eggplant, garlic, tomato, turmeric. Char the eggplant hard over open flame; that smoke *is* the dish.
- **هلیم** (halim) — wheat berry porridge.
- **پلو هویج** (polo havij) — carrot rice with raisins, peas, warm spices.
- **ته‌دیگ** (tahdig) — the crisped rice crust at the bottom of the pot. The single most fought-over thing on any Iranian table. Potato slices make the most forgiving base.

## 10. The Opposition and the Question of "After"

- **Reza Pahlavi** — the most prominent opposition figure. Has committed publicly to **free elections and a people's referendum** once the regime falls. His transition framework / "Emergency Booklet" covers interim governance, dissolution of the IRGC, nuclear transparency, and a referendum on the form of government.
- **Masih Alinejad** — activist, mobilizes international attention, target of multiple IRGC assassination plots on US soil.
- **Narges Mohammadi** — Nobel laureate, inside the country, imprisoned.

**An important framing correction preserved from this material:** avoid phrasing that implies Iranians lack a ready alternative or need to "figure it out once free." That reads as an argument for keeping the regime. The opposition exists and has published plans. The problem is not Iranian unreadiness; the problem is that people need to survive long enough to get there.

### Expert landscape on regime collapse (as of late March 2026)
Useful for making the game's political layer honest rather than wishful:
- **Majority view: the regime survives, weakened.** Linda Robinson (CFR): "Taking out the Supreme Leader is not the same as regime change. The IRGC is the regime." Aaron Stein (FPRI): "The IRGC has that place wired. They won't go quietly." Maj. Gen. (res.) Tamir Hayman (INSS) calls collapse "extremely unlikely."
- **Hawkish minority:** Michael Doran (Hudson): no scenario where the Islamic Republic survives 2026 with its power intact. Mark Dubowitz (FDD): "the most consequential opening in 47 years." Nate Swanson (Atlantic Council): parallels 1979, which took a year to unfold.
- **Polymarket priced regime fall in 2026 at ~16%** in early January, before the war.
- **The paradox** (Danny Citrinowicz, INSS): an agreement prevents a bomb but strengthens the regime; a strike weakens the regime but hardens its resolve to get one. Vali Nasr: with Khamenei dead, his fatwa against nuclear weapons is no longer binding.
- **Ali Vaez (Crisis Group), March 26:** "Trump has started a war he now cannot end."

## 11. The Diaspora Layer

- Watching a massacre happen in a country you cannot reach, on a feed that has gone dark.
- **Calling in during a blackout.** Outgoing calls from Iran work; incoming calls are blocked or routed to a nonsensical pre-recorded English message. Landlines connect more often than mobiles. Standard calling cards (Rebtel, YOLO) fail. Workarounds that have worked: **Mytello** (German, routes over traditional telephony rather than VoIP) and **TamasbaVatan/KeepCalling** (US). Use dial-in mode, not VoIP. Target landlines. Call off-peak.
- **Choosing services carefully** — the fear that a calling or messaging service is regime-connected and is logging who the diaspora talks to.
- **Donating bandwidth** as the one concrete thing you can do from outside: run a Psiphon Conduit node on a spare device.
- **The disclosure dilemma.** Many Iranian-Americans deliberately do not disclose their connection to Iran publicly — professionally, on LinkedIn, in public writing — because of how dual nationals have been treated in the news and at borders. Authority has to come from what you know, not from who you are. This tension is a real and underexplored story element.

## 12. The Arguments the Game Should Be Able to Survive

Real counterarguments encountered, and the real responses. Build NPCs out of these; a game that can't hold its own against the best version of the opposing case isn't worth playing.

**"Criticizing the Islamic Republic is Islamophobia."**
→ "Islamic Republic" is the actual, self-chosen name of the government. Objecting to the name is objecting to a fact. There is also a distinction between a Muslim and an Islamist. And Iran is the country where the entire legal code, including the constitution, is built on religious law — so religion is not an outside topic here, it's the subject.

**"The US and Israel will just make it another Iraq or Libya."**
→ A serious argument deserving a serious answer, not dismissal. The response given: Iran differs from Iraq and Libya in several respects — an existing organized opposition with a published transition plan, a large educated urban population, strong pre-existing national identity, and a diaspora with institutional capacity. But the concern about collective punishment is legitimate and is exactly why the "target the regime, not the nation" line exists.

**"Fix your own country first before judging others."**
→ Deflection. Also: the person saying this has never lived under the regime being discussed.

**"The regime responded to the protests peacefully."**
→ Factually false, and the point at which a conversation ends.

**"Iranians need to defend themselves from US/Israeli attack."**
→ The regime is not Iran's defender; it is the thing most Iranians want removed. Conflating the two is the regime's own framing, repeated by people who think they're being anti-imperialist.

**The Palestine question.**
→ A specific and raw source of anger: the Islamic Republic sends enormous sums to Hamas and Hezbollah to maintain regional power projection, while Iranians go without. Western progressives who mobilize intensely for Gaza have been largely silent on Iranian protesters killed in the streets. This asymmetry — being told constantly about one cause by people who will not say a word about yours — is a defining diaspora grievance.

## 13. Themes Worth Building the Game Around

1. **Connection as a resource.** Bandwidth, a working config, a phone call that goes through — these are scarce, consumable, and worth risking your life for.
2. **The censor as an adversary with rules.** Not an invisible wall. A system with inspection depth, a whitelist, a state table, and blind spots. Players should be able to learn and exploit it.
3. **The double life.** A public-persona meter and a private-self meter that must be managed separately, with penalties when they leak into each other.
4. **Memory as resistance.** "When we're free tomorrow, let's not forget what we endured." Collecting, preserving, and transmitting names and evidence.
5. **Nothing works cleanly.** Pulsing connections, apps that die mid-call, routes that work Tuesday and not Wednesday.
6. **Joy as a mechanic.** Setting a Haft-Sin, cooking, music, poetry — small acts that restore something the oppression takes, and that are themselves punishable.
7. **Nobody is coming.** The absence of rescue as a structural fact of the world.
8. **Inheritance.** What a civilization keeps when a state tries to overwrite it: a language, an epic, a calendar tuned to the sun.

## 14. Editorial Rules for Contributors and AI Assistants

**Do:**
- Say "the Islamic Republic," "the regime," "the IRGC." Not "Iran" when you mean the government.
- Use Persian terms with transliteration and translation on first use.
- Verify any Shahnameh or classical verse against Ganjoor before shipping it.
- Hold Pahlavi-era history honestly — achievements *and* SAVAK.
- Keep grief and defiance in the same frame.
- Write Persian that favors Persian-root vocabulary over Arabic loanwords.

**Don't:**
- Don't fabricate verses, translations, or quotes and attribute them to a real source. This is the one unrecoverable error.
- Don't flatten Iranians into victims. They have fought, repeatedly, at enormous cost.
- Don't treat "reformists" as a good-faith alternative within this project's frame.
- Don't use the unverified low casualty figures.
- Don't write Iranian culture as generic "Middle Eastern." Persian is its own language family branch, its own calendar, its own literary canon, its own new year.
- Don't present Islam-the-faith and the Islamic Republic-the-state as the same object, even though this project is sharply critical of the latter's legal foundation.
- Don't write Nowruz, Yalda, Chaharshanbe Suri, or Mehregan as quaint folklore. They are 3,000-year-old observances that a state has actively tried to suppress.
