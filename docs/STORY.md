# Story

## Kaveh Lane

Dusk in a fictional Iranian neighborhood. Someone has spread a rug in the
courtyard. Friends have brought music and are waiting for you. The street's
power is out, and patrols still pass through.

Restore power at three points around the lane, then return to your friends.
Windows brighten as you go. At the end, string lights come on, the patrols
leave, and the gathering begins. You can stay. Reclaiming ordinary life is
the reward.

Kaveh's resistance to Zahhak in the Shahnameh inspires the street's name and
an original banner. This is a contemporary fictional setting, not a retelling
of the conquest of Iran or a reconstruction of a real historic neighborhood.

## The larger game

Iranian life should fill the world: homes, bread shops, gardens, rooftops,
poetry, humor, music, friendship, romance, and pride in the country's history.
Use protest graffiti, masked protesters, Persian chants, and recognizable
political figures as the cast and art develop. The current people are
placeholders; realistic characters and a full political cast are still ahead.

The pressure comes from control over ordinary life: forced dress rules,
restrictions on mixed gatherings, drinking, expression, belief, and queer
relationships; gender separation; economic strain; and fear of detention
and violence. Show the impact on people, including queer and trans people,
without making their suffering a punchline. Sexual violence stays off-screen.

Let characters have their own views on the Pahlavi era, religion, the West,
and Iran's future. Satire targets abusive power. Love of Iran is expressed
through people and places.

Future places: a private party, a bar, a rooftop escape, a shuttered cafe, a transit
stop, a workplace, and a street protest. Add weapon variety after encounters
feel good.

## Words and references

Use native Persian vocabulary rather than Arabic loanwords wherever Persian
is spoken or written. Review uncertain words with the creator. The opening
uses زن، زندگی، آزادی, کوچه کاوه, نان, شاهنامه, and کنار هم.
English remains available for controls and gameplay instructions.

The creator particularly loves the Shahnameh. Other requested references
include Two Centuries of Silence, Persepolis, Iran's ancient history,
constitutional history, and Zoroastrian traditions. See [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)
for sources and the book list. Depict a specific story rather than treating
every historical interpretation as settled fact.
