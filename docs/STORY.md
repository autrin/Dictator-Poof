# Story

## Nowruz: the first visit

The game opens at home on the morning of the family celebration. Open the
blinds onto three cypresses, honeysuckle, fruit trees, and birdsong. Mum has
chosen a Nowruz music program; the Haft-Seen is already set. Your sister has
saved her blue outfit for today.

On the way to your grandparents, a morality patrol stops her outside the
pastry shop and demands she cover herself. Stay beside her, collect the dry
and cream pastries, and continue the visit. At Grandma's, relatives share
ajil, fruit, stories, and Sal Tahvil. Help with sabzi polo and fish, have lunch,
then play a hand of Hokm before returning home and joining the next meal.

The café is an optional stop for coffee, books, and conversation. It also
appears in Kaveh Lane. Ramadan restrictions are disabled during this chapter;
the separate service/enforcement rules are groundwork for a later story.

This is an authored fictional day, without an assigned historical year or
verified equinox timetable. Family members follow authored walking routes and
ride in the moving car, with the player looking around from the back seat.
The patrol arrives by van and approaches on foot; its dialogue identifies the
visible officer. Cooking and eating use short character animations, and the
evening visit reuses Grandma's house. These are choreographed scenes rather
than a traffic or cooking simulation. Hokm
uses four players, partnerships, suit-following and trump, ending at seven
tricks for one team; a full seven-point match is not implemented. Progress is
session-only. The character models and environments remain stylized.

## Kaveh Lane

After the dinner visit, your aunt points you to a note your cousin left on the
blue Kaveh Lane gate. He went ahead to the courtyard gathering. The note warns
that armed guards have cut the power and are firing on people, and identifies
a pistol case beside the gate. Reading it does not end the family visit: taking
the case and entering the lane is a separate interaction. The family stays
indoors while you go to your friends. This fictional raid connects the two
chapters; the original night mission is also available from the title screen.

Night follows the family visit; the standalone mission starts at dusk.
In this fictional Iranian neighborhood, someone has spread a rug in the
courtyard. Friends have brought music and are waiting for you. The street's
power is out, and patrols still pass through.

Restore power at three points around the lane, then return to your friends.
Windows brighten as you go. At the end, string lights come on, the patrols
leave, and the gathering begins. You can stay. Reclaiming ordinary life is
the reward.

Beyond the north passage, friends hold a private party in Mahtab's courtyard
house. They dance, share wine, and joke, but the host warns that anyone tied
to the authorities could expose them to a morality patrol raid.

Kaveh's resistance to Zahhak in the Shahnameh inspires the street's name and
an original banner. This is a contemporary fictional setting, not a retelling
of the conquest of Iran or a reconstruction of a real historic neighborhood.

## The larger game

Iranian life should fill the world: homes, bread shops, gardens, rooftops,
poetry, humor, music, friendship, romance, and pride in the country's history.
Use protest graffiti, masked protesters, Persian chants, and recognizable
political figures as the cast and art develop. The current people are stylized
procedural characters; realistic characters and a full political cast are still ahead.

The pressure comes from control over ordinary life: forced dress rules,
restrictions on mixed gatherings, drinking, expression, belief, and queer
relationships; gender separation; economic strain; and fear of detention
and violence. Show the impact on people, including queer and trans people,
without making their suffering a punchline. Sexual violence stays off-screen.

Let characters have their own views on the Pahlavi era, religion, the West,
and Iran's future. Satire targets abusive power. Love of Iran is expressed
through people and places.

Future places: a bar, a rooftop escape, a shuttered cafe, a transit
stop, a workplace, and a street protest. Add weapon variety after encounters
feel good.

## Words and references

Use native Persian vocabulary rather than Arabic loanwords wherever Persian
is spoken or written. Review uncertain words with the creator. The opening
uses زن، زندگی، آزادی, کوچه کاوه, نان, شاهنامه, and کنار هم.
The playable text can be switched between Persian and English during the game.

The creator particularly loves the Shahnameh. Other requested references
include Two Centuries of Silence, Persepolis, Iran's ancient history,
constitutional history, and Zoroastrian traditions. See [RESEARCH.md](RESEARCH.md)
for sources and the book list. Depict a specific story rather than treating
every historical interpretation as settled fact.
